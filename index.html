Position

Order, rupture and the template.

The grid is the plan. The cats are what actually happens. This page is the space between them.

What is ordered

My life runs on a grid. Two kids, four cats, Ben, a design master's degree: none of it works without a timetable. School pickup is a column. Feeding time is a row. I bake by feel weight, not by guessing, and a Saint-Honoré is six hours of steps in exactly the right order. That part of me is Swiss. So the backbone of this page is Swiss too: twelve columns, a 24px gutter, an 8px baseline, one grotesque typeface in six sizes, black, white and one red. I chose every one of those numbers and wrote them in the margin, so you can check me.

What breaks it

Sofi knocks the plan off the table. Tova sleeps belly up in the middle of the schedule. The Saint-Honoré takes six hours and is gone in six minutes. Poppy seeds get into everything: the dough, the keyboard, this website. The windows, the camera roll, the photo of me at 7am and the one of me asleep are not decoration. They are the parts of my life that no grid has ever held and no model could have guessed. They don't sit next to the order. They land on it, cover it and pull it out of line, because that is how it happens at home.

Against templatology

Ask an AI for a personal site and you get Exhibit A: a centered hero, a purple gradient, three rounded cards, Inter, and a sentence about crafting delightful experiences. It looks organised, but nobody made a decision. The radius is 16px because everyone's is. The copy fits every designer, so it describes none. Müller-Brockmann's grid was a belief about clarity and honesty. The template is that belief with the belief taken out. So I put it first, labelled every default in red, and crossed it out by hand. Then I built the real order after it, stricter than the template, with a reason for every rule.

The encounter

I don't think either side wins. A day where the plan survives untouched is perfect boring. A day with no plan is a disaster. My identity is the moment in between, when a window is dragged off its column and I can still see the column underneath. That is why the grid lines stay visible everywhere on this page, even under the mess, and why every window you drag snaps back to a column while keeping its tilt. The rupture never erases the order. The order is what makes the rupture readable, and the rupture is what proves a person lives here.
